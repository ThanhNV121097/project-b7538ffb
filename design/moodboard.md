# Moodboard — Tony Apple

Store: genuine Apple accessories, 19 Duy Tân, Hà Nội. No owner reference link was given, so the direction is built from current product-retail and consumer-tech sites.

## Sites watched

1. **apple.com/shop/buy-iphone** — the category it sells into. Huge confident sans headlines (SF Pro), generous white space, product renders as the only imagery, sticky filter/compare bar, horizontal model carousel, cards that stack into a soft-grey canvas. Take: product-as-hero, short declarative headlines ("Fast that lasts."), a horizontal scroller for the catalogue.
2. **nothing.tech** — black ground, huge serif-ish display type over a product photo, a small eyebrow label ("HEADPHONE (1) PRO"), Lenis smooth scroll, dense top utility bar. Take: dark ground with a single product lit dramatically behind the headline; eyebrow label as a structural device (category tag, not decoration).
3. **nomadgoods.com** (Nomad — premium iPhone cases, watch bands, chargers; closest direct competitor in category) — black hero band with bold condensed sans, a 3-up photo hero (band photographed on a wrist/in the field), sticky nav, dense "Shop by Category" tile grid below the fold, large flat product photography on light grey cards. Take: the category-tile grid as the main navigation device, bold flush-left condensed headline type, product photographed in use (hand, wrist) not just on white.
4. **land-book.com** (gallery, for current grid/typography norms) — Inter throughout, tight editorial grid, large sticky filter bar, cards that reveal on scroll. Take: confidence of a tight 12-col grid and restrained one-font-family discipline when the type itself does the work.

## Direction for Tony Apple

Dark ground (near-black charcoal, titanium-inspired) carrying warm brass/copper accents — not Apple's own white-and-blue, so the store reads as its own premium boutique rather than a copy of Apple's site, while the material language (brushed metal, glass, precise type) still says "Apple accessories." Display type is **Syne** (geometric, wide, a little unexpected — it gives headlines a distinct silhouette the way Nothing's dot-matrix mark does, without aping it) paired with **Inter** for every running line, so the page reads instantly as tech-retail but not generic SaaS. Motion language: a load sequence that splits and raises the hero headline line by line (SplitType + GSAP), a horizontal marquee of category tags under the hero (Nomad's category-tile habit, turned into motion), a pinned product showcase that steps through four accessory categories as the page scrolls (GSAP ScrollTrigger pin, echoing Apple's sticky compare bar), parallax drift on the product photography, Lenis for the scroll feel itself, and small magnetic hover lift on every card and button.
